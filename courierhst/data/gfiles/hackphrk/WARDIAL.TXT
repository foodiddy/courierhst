  ::: THE NIGHT I DIALED ALL OF HICKSVILLE :::
      a true(ish) story by Pixel Poet, age 15 at the time

  It started in the summer of '91. I had a 2400 baud modem, a free
  weekend and parents who were at my aunt's. Perfect storm.

  The idea was simple. Our town had one exchange, 555, and every number in
  the 01xx block belonged to someone. So I wrote a little program in BASIC
  that dialed the numbers one by one and wrote down which ones answered
  with a funny noise instead of a person.

  Rules I set myself: never call after 10 pm; hang up on anybody who says
  hello; never touch anything that wasn't mine to touch. That last one turns
  out to be the most important.

  Here is the log from my notebook:

     555-0101   a person (hung up fast, felt bad)
     555-0104   answering machine, a man singing opera
     555-0111   a very angry dog
     555-0123   MODEM! it was Dr. Ledger's dental office, billing system.
                I said nothing and went away. (Took a long shower after.)
     555-0131   a fax. It screamed at me for thirty seconds.
     555-0142   A BBS!! The Wire Cellar, run by a guy called Cipher Moth.
     555-0150   nobody home
     555-0166   the school attendance computer. It asked for a password.
                I know that password is not "password" because I tried
                it, and I was wrong, and I got scared and logged out.
     555-0177   Tony's Pizza. I ordered a large pepperoni.

  The Wire Cellar was the real prize. I called it at 2400 baud and got
  a blue login screen, a message that said "Welcome, new user!", and a
  menu with files and messages. Within a week I was in the scene, and
  within a month I had a handle.

  The attendance computer worried me, though. I wrote an anonymous note and
  put it on the principal's desk: "Your attendance machine answers the phone.
  Please give it a password a kid can't guess." They fixed it by Friday and
  nobody ever asked who wrote the note. The principal's name was Mr. Gruber
  and he thanked me, years later, at a school reunion.

  What I learned, in one line: the interesting thing about a phone number
  isn't who picks up. It's what they do when you tell them kindly.

  Total cost of the weekend: one pizza, one scared stomach and a lifelong
  hobby. Would do again, with more cheese.
